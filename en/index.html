THE QUESTION BEHIND EAI

When does using AI add meaning?

The text is written. The analysis is ready. The lesson has been prepared. AI may have saved considerable time. But what has actually changed? Did someone learn something, make a better judgement, or simply hand over part of the work?

EAI starts with the purpose of the work and the actions needed to achieve it. Sometimes handing those actions to AI makes perfect sense. When learning is the goal, the same assistance may remove something a student needs to learn to do.

How EAI developed →See an example
WHY IT MATTERS

What can the result actually tell us?

When we look only at the result, we may overlook the thinking that was skipped. Sometimes that is helpful. Sometimes that thinking was the entire point.

The question is where AI should help, why, and with what consequences.

A PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

What changes when the purpose changes?

Suppose AI writes an analysis. For someone preparing a decision, that may be useful. For someone learning to analyse, it may remove the very work they need to practise.

POSSIBLE CHOICE

The goal is a usable analysis. AI may prepare most of it, while a person checks whether the result is suitable.

How the EAI question developed.

EAI did not start with a new tool. It started with an observation: AI can make work easier while making it harder to see what people still understand, practise and decide for themselves.

THE EAI MODEL

Start with what people do, not what AI can do.

To assess AI in a meaningful way, first identify the human actions that matter. Work from the full process down to its individual steps.

  1. 01

    Process

    Which work or learning process are we examining?

  2. 02

    Phase

    What are its distinct phases?

  3. 03

    Core action

    Which action must the learner make sense of themselves at this stage in order to learn?

  4. 04

    AI

    Where can AI help, and where would it take over an essential step?

The answer depends on the goal. Outsourcing a routine task may help an expert, while a student may need to practise that very task.
